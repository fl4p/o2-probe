## Why the monobody failed, and what changed

It came off the plate at **layer 928 of 1221** (Z 111.4 of 146.6 mm), ~3h40m
into a 4h30m print. The brim lifted **partially** — one side — which is the
signature of a directional thermal gradient, and the machine was in direct sun.
Contributing, but not the cause: it was standing on its own **219 mm²** end face
because `brim_object_gap` was Bambu's default 0.1, so 581 mm² of brim was parked
beside the part rather than attached to it.

Changed since:

- Brim settings are now **per part** (`part_process_overrides()` in `print.sh`),
  because the parts disagree: the monobody wants a fused brim inside and out;
  the carrier's z=0 **is** the membrane sealing land and any fused brim scars it.
- `print.sh` generates `print/<part>/process.json` — the settings that actually
  sliced a plate now sit next to the plate.
- It **refuses** to override `layer_height`, `initial_layer_print_height` or
  `outer_wall_line_width` per part, because the CAD quantizes seal dimensions
  against those and `tools/profile_check.py` gates them against the base profile.
- `tools/seal_check.sh` now **requires `--part`**: the correct brim values are
  opposite for the monobody and the carrier and nothing in the G-code says which
  part it is, so an unnamed part is unevaluable and fails.

## Traps that cost time today, in the order they will bite again

1. **A plate sliced before its STL was rebuilt.** Happened twice. Check the
   mtime chain `.scad → STL → plate` before trusting any plate. It looks
   perfect and slices clean.
2. **`curl` always exits 28 on a Bambu upload.** A refused write is
   indistinguishable from a good one. **Verify by listing the card.**
3. **A Bambu fault names the machine's hypothesis, not its measurement** — but
   test before dismissing it. Today `0x0500C010` "replace the MicroSD card" was
   simply correct, and an all-clear taken 4 minutes earlier was already stale.
4. **A part rendered bare may not be in the assembly frame.** `part="cap"` draws
   24.3 mm low; `part="cap-asm"` is the assembly one. Fab caught this by eye; no
   check did.
5. **`part="cap-asm"` renders EMPTY without `face_sorting="sorted"`** — it trips
   the face-seal assert at line 1027, whose message never mentions the cap. Use
   `make print-ASA`, never hand-rolled `-D`.
6. **A calibration ends in `FINISH`, not `IDLE`**, and stays there.

## Open, unresolved, honest

- ~~`cell_axial_len` — unmeasured.~~ **Closed, not by measuring it: the cell mount
  went back to standing (see item 1). `cell_seated_h = 8.8` is the placeholder
  that replaces it, and it no longer sets the head length.**
- **`fast_hole_h` can set `cav_board_len`, and when it does it is expensive.**
  With the neck gone, the retainer screws are the only thing left that stops the
  head getting shorter: they enter from the end face, go `fast_hole_h` deep, and
  sit on a bolt circle that overlaps the bonded socket, so the model solves
  `cav_board_len = max(2.0, fast_hole_h + 2 - cav_clear_len - spigot_len)`.
  That bore is **466 mm³/mm**, the most expensive millimetre in the probe.
  **It is not binding right now** — a parallel session moved the fastener to M2
  the same evening (`fast_hole_h = 2*pt_nominal + pt_cbore_h` = 5.0), which puts
  the solve at 1.7 and the floor of 2.0 wins. At the old M2.5 / 8.0 mm it solved
  to 4.7 and cost **1.26 cm³**. So: *any* future change to the screw, its
  counterbore or the spigot length can silently lengthen the head again.
  **`tools/interface_check.py` is what catches it** — the body model's
  `cav_board_len` and the head's `body_tube_len` are hand copies of a value that
  is now derived, and they went stale in both directions within one hour today.
  Run it after any fastener change.
