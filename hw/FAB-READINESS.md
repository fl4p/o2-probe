# O2 probe fabrication-readiness gate

Status date: 2026-08-24

## Decision

**Ready for a bare-board first article at a capability-matched fabricator.**
**Not ready to source parts from: `audit_bom.py` reports FAIL.** Bare boards are
unaffected, but 16 fitted components still carry a value family instead of an
orderable MPN, and the Rev A purchase orders are already three parts short
because of it. See `bom-audit.rpt` before building any basket.
The selected process must accept 0.09/0.15 mm trace/space, 0.25/0.15 mm
via/drill, 0.20 mm copper-to-routed-edge clearance, two plated-slot edge
castellations, all four internal thermal slots, and
a 1.00 mm four-layer stack. This is not an automated-PCBA,
heated-ring assembly, or production-qualified release. Real SGX fit, enclosure
fit, ring mechanics, and environmental qualification remain mandatory.

The orderable artifact is generated by `make_fab.py` at
`fab/o2-probe-rev-a-fab.zip`. The builder runs ERC, connectivity audits, error
and warning DRC, schematic parity, known-bad calibration, exact Gerber/Excellon
validation, and the procurement audit. The last one does not gate bare boards,
but its verdict is stamped into `fab-manifest.json` under `bom` and into the
manifest's own `qualification_scope`, so a release can no longer describe itself
as ready while its parts are unbuyable.

## Closed fabrication blockers

  336 assigned pins, and 92 nets.
- KiCad PCB DRC has 0 errors and 0 unconnected items. The release does not
  rely on KiCad 10.0.5's parity result: `audit_pcb.py` independently compares
  the exact schematic XML reference set and every numbered PCB pad net, with
  zero mismatches on the release.
- CELL_RE remains one 9.652 mm, 0.15 mm F.Cu segment from U7.13 to U11.2,
  without a via or test point. Four exact stitch vias and ten short In1/B.Cu
  links retain three physically connected `GUARD_REPLICA` fill regions on both
  sides of the long run; the audit checks the saved filled polygons, not only
  the zone source outline.
- The board is exactly 20.00 × 107.00 × 1.00 mm with four copper layers, an
  exact six-primitive outline with R2 cable-end corners and a square gas end,
  and four exact thermal-slot contours. The two 6.75 × 1.50 mm horizontal
  slots are centred at `(104.125,134.00)` and `(115.875,134.00)` mm and retain
  0.75 mm edge webs plus a 5.00 mm centre neck. The two 0.80 × 16.00 mm

  span y=137.00–153.00 mm, and have no track, via, or pad crossing.
- U11's separate 3.00 × 6.00 mm gasward NPTH oblong is centred at absolute
  `(110.00,145.30)` mm, 0.50 mm from pad-2 copper. The release validates its
  tool, exact route endpoints, pad gap, and the rerouted inner-layer heater
  feed independently.
- The obsolete U13/C22/R30/TP7 second-temperature circuit and both of its
  dedicated nets are absent from the schematic, PCB, and release.
- J5 is exactly two 2.00 × 3.00 mm through-hole corner pads at the left/right
  gas-side edges. Their 1.00 × 2.00 mm plated slots carry KiCad's castellated

- `HEATER_LOW` uses only ≥0.80 mm B.Cu tracks in the tip and a solid B.Cu

## Reviewed warnings and scope

KiCad reports 20 courtyard overlaps. Their exact pair set is locked in
`audit_pcb.py` and listed in `ASSEMBLY-EXCEPTIONS.md`; they are hand-assembly
review items, not bare-board errors. The remaining 33 legend warnings are 18
silk overlaps, 14 silk-over-copper findings, and one silk-to-edge finding on
layers intentionally omitted from the Gerber release. There are no waivers for
copper, mask, holes, outline, connectivity, or schematic parity.

### The stencil layers are now exported; silkscreen is a waiver

`make_fab.py` plots `F.Paste` and `B.Paste` as of 2026-09-04. They were absent
before, and nothing recorded a decision to leave them out -- which is the whole
problem, because the footprints carry real paste engineering that was being
discarded in silence. `Texas_RTE0016D` (U8) is the case that found it: its
exposed pad gets copper and mask only, and four separate 0.32 x 0.32 mm
apertures supply 60.6 % of the pad area in a segmented pattern, specifically so
the part cannot float on molten paste and starve its perimeter joints. With no
paste layer in the release, an assembler cuts a stencil from the copper, which
on that pad is the 1:1 aperture the footprint was drawn to avoid. Verified by
plotting: the four apertures reach the Gerber as D16, flashed at the four
expected coordinates.

Silkscreen stays out, but as a **waiver with a lifting condition** rather than an
omission. The 33 legend warnings above are harmless only while nothing is
printed; 14 of them put silk on exposed copper, where it degrades solderability.
Lift the waiver when those are cleared -- several offenders (RV1, RH1, RH2,
R20-R23) are deleted by the pending AFE/fork revision, so the cleanup gets
cheaper by waiting. `make_fab.py` refuses to build if the waiver and the plotted
layer set disagree, and both manifest booleans are now derived from `LAYERS`
rather than hardcoded, so the manifest cannot claim silkscreen was withheld
while the Gerbers contain it.

**These are source-level changes and the current release does not have them.**
`fab/o2-probe-rev-a/` and its ZIP were generated before any of this: they still
contain only copper, mask and Edge.Cuts, and their manifest has neither the
paste layers nor `silkscreen_waiver`. The presently orderable artifact therefore
still carries the original defect. Nothing here is fixed for a fabricator until
`make_fab.py` is re-run, which cannot happen while the board is mid-revision.

### The test-point labels are on the board and not in the Gerbers

`gen_pcb.py` prints each test point's function next to its pad -- GUARD, WE, CE,
VREF on F.Silkscreen and TMP_CELL on B.Silkscreen -- and `audit_pcb.py` enforces
that every test point carries exactly one legible, unambiguous label. They add no
DRC finding of any kind: the 33 legend warnings above are unchanged by them.

They will not reach a physical board. `make_fab.py` plots
`F.Cu, In1.Cu, In2.Cu, B.Cu, F.Mask, B.Mask, F.Paste, B.Paste, Edge.Cuts` (the
released ZIP predates the paste layers) and records
`silkscreen_exported: false`; the labels appear only in the drawing PDF. Printing
them is a separate decision with a real cost attached, because the silkscreen
layer cannot be plotted selectively: adding `F.Silkscreen,B.Silkscreen` to
`make_fab.LAYERS` also prints the 33 warnings above, and those stop being
cosmetic once they are ink -- 14 of them put silk on exposed copper, where it
degrades solderability. Clearing them is a footprint-silk cleanup across the
whole board, not a test-point change, and several of the offenders (RV1, RH1,
RH2, R20-R23) are deleted by the pending AFE/fork revision anyway. Decide it in
that revision, not before.

## First-article acceptance gates

These do not block ordering bare boards, but they do block production:

- Fit a real SGX-OX-ROHS-Mini, gauge its pins, verify PCB-side CE/RE/WE, body
  standoff, and unobstructed gas faces around the 3 × 6 mm aperture.
- Freeze the ring drawing, alloy/plating, thickness, joint geometry, membrane
  clamping method, and electrical insulation. J5 is switched `HEATER_LOW`, not
  ground; no ring metal may reach the wet face, enclosure, or accessible parts.
- Coupon the 20 × 107 mm board, ring, PTFE membrane, carrier, potting dam, and
  connector in the actual 32 mm tube. Measure the final O₂-to-membrane spacing
  rather than inferring it from the PCB outline.
- Confirm the selected fabricator plates and profiles the two J5 castellations
  as documented; inspect sidewall continuity and copper adhesion on coupons.
- Hand assemble and inspect the reviewed courtyard pairs and the J5 solder
  operation.
  Do not submit the bare-board ZIP for turnkey automated PCBA.
- Run heater open/short/stuck-on protection tests and map ring, membrane, U10,
  U12, SGX body, and ambient temperatures at 0/intermediate/100% drive.

### Heater bench results, 2026-09-04 (first article, RH1/RH2 fitted, no ring)

First energisation of the heater on real hardware. `O2P_HEATER_ENABLE` stays
**0** in the shipped build; this ran from `env:o2probe_heater_enabled`.

**The TMP117 alert fault test passes, on its register evidence.** Read back
from the part before the test: `CONFIG = 0x21B0` (therm mode, active-low,
500 ms, 8x averaging - bit-for-bit the firmware's intent), `THIGH = 0x2A80` =
**85.00 C**, `TLOW = 0x2800` = **80.00 C**. Writing `THIGH` down to 30.00 C
against a 35.4 C tip asserted `HIGH_Alert` on the next conversion.

The test was run with the **core halted**, which is stronger than the
procedure above asks for: `DBGMCU_APB_FZ2 = 0`, so TIM1 keeps generating PWM
while the CPU is stopped. `TIM1 CR1 = 0x81, CCER = 0x01, CCR1 = 1462/16000`
were unchanged across the alert, so the MCU was provably still commanding
9.14 % duty and could not have been what turned the heater off.

Also measured:

- **The rail monitor survives the heater running.** `TIM1_CCER` bit CC1NE read
  0 with the timer live, so PA7 is not being driven as TIM1_CH1N. This is the
  hardware form of `o2p_heater_pin_integrity_ok()`, not the flag's own word.
- **PWM frequency is exact**: ARR 15999, PSC 0 at 16 MHz = 1000.0 Hz.
- **The load is real**: VIN_MON fell 11960 -> 11914 mV between 0 % and 93 %
  duty, ~46 mV for the predicted ~80 mA, implying ~0.58 ohm of supply and lead
  resistance. Bench PSU resolution did not show it; the divider did.
- **The loop closes.** Duty clamped at `HEATER_MAX_DUTY`, then regulated down
  19.5 -> 6.2 % as chamber RH fell through setpoint, tip settling near 33.9 C.
  No runaway, no fault but `SETTLING`.
- **Detect and recover.** Firmware caught the externally changed `THIGH` within
  its 10 s re-verification, disarmed, and the interlock forced duty to 0
  (`faults = 0x8009`). Restoring 85.00/80.00 C cleared `HIGH_Alert` and
  re-armed within one cycle (`faults = 0x0001`).
- **The cell is unharmed** by an excursion that peaked at 56.1 C: 100.71 uA and
  21.06 %vol afterwards, against 100.66 uA / 21.02 %vol before. Raw current
  moved under 0.1 % across a ~14 K sweep (~36 ppm/K), which is the first
  empirical support for the `tempco_ppm_per_k = 0` default - suggestive of an
  internally compensated cell, not a calibrated tempco measurement.

**The cutoff is closed THERMALLY, and no scope was needed.** `DBGMCU_APB_FZ2`
is 0 (read with `RCC_APBENR1` DBGEN actually set - with the DBGMCU clock gated
the register reads 0 whatever it holds, which is a trap), so TIM1 keeps
generating PWM while the core is halted. Freezing only the IWDG
(`DBGMCU_APB_FZ1` bit 12; without it the 4.1 s watchdog resets the part
mid-test) allows a 30 s halt:

```
t= 0s  CCR1=8000/16000  tip=40.46 C  alert=0    <- 50 % duty commanded
       THIGH -> 30.00 C, core HALTED throughout
t= 3s  CCR1=8000        tip=40.85 C  alert=1
t=30s  CCR1=8000        tip=35.59 C  alert=1
```

The board **cooled 5.3 K in 30 s while the timer still demanded 50 % duty and
the CPU was stopped**. Only the open-drain ALERT can remove that heat. This is
better evidence than the trace the procedure asks for: it shows the physical
outcome rather than a gate voltage, with firmware provably absent.

**Closed by measurement, not by scope:**

- *Vgs adequacy.* The amplitude is a divider - 3.3 V x 100k/110k = 3.0 V from
  R28/R29 as the contract asserts them - and the question that matters is
  whether it enhances the FET, which ~80 mA at 93 % duty answers directly.
- *FET dissipation at 1 kHz.* Bounded by the circuit: the FET is in series with
  140 ohm on 12 V, so the whole loop can only dissipate 1.03 W, and conduction
  loss is under a milliwatt at any plausible Rds(on).

**Still open:**

- ~~`heater_pwm_hz` validated to 20 kHz~~ **fixed in c469664**: the ceiling is
  now 2 kHz, with 20000 pinned as rejected by test so a quiet revert fails.
  The gate EDGE TIME is still unverified - it has never been on a scope - so
  raising the ceiling again remains a bench measurement, not an edit.
- Everything ring- and membrane-side. No ring was fitted. **The heat source
  stays on the main board**: an earlier Rev B pass deleted RH1/RH2 and moved it
  to a ring PCB, and that was reverted on 2026-09-05, so the 140 ohm and 1.02 W
  above are live figures rather than Rev A history.

**`dt_max_k` is an inert interlock, and its unit test cannot see that.** The
config carries a 4 K tip-above-chamber clamp and
`test_dt_clamp_binds_and_has_hysteresis` passes - on synthetic inputs. On this
board U10 (TMP117, tip) and U12 (SHT45, chamber) sit 6.6 mm apart on the same
front-layer copper, both behind J5: `gen_pcb.py` places them at (10.0, 102.525)
and (3.5, 101.5). They are one thermal node. Measured across a 30 K excursion,
|tip - chamber| stayed within 0.4 K at every duty from 0 to 100 %, with the tip
marginally the COOLER of the two:

```
duty  50 %   tip 31.06  chamber 31.43   -0.37 K
duty  93 %   tip 43.49  chamber 43.87   -0.38 K
duty 100 %   tip 56.10  chamber 56.24   -0.14 K
```

So the clamp can never bind on real hardware, and no ring would change that -
the two sensors cannot disagree by construction. This is the same shape as the
DRDY defect: a fixture proving the design we intended rather than the one we
built. Real tip overtemperature protection is `TEMP_OVER` at 60 C and the
TMP117 cutoff at 85 C, both verified above, so nothing is unprotected - but
`dt_max_k` should be either deleted or re-pointed at two sensors that are
actually thermally separated.

**The runaway detector had the same blindness, and that mattered more.**
`HEATER_RUNAWAY` latched on tip-minus-chamber, so the detector written to catch
a stuck-on FET could not see one: a stuck FET heats the whole tip assembly and
both sensors rise together. Fixed in **7b021f5** by adding a die-referenced
test alongside the chamber one - the MCU die sits across the transverse
barrier and does not follow the tip (-2.2 K at rest, +2.1 K at 4 % duty, and
tip 56 C against die 28 C at a stuck 100 %). Its +/-5 C absolute accuracy is
useless for a 4 K clamp but irrelevant against tens of kelvin.

The 10.00 K threshold is **provisional**: 3.4x the widest legitimate zero-duty
excursion measured, catching stuck duty above roughly 12 % in still air. A pile
couples far more tightly and the same fault gives a smaller rise, so
`PILE-THERMAL-TEST.md` is what should replace it. The dT clamp is kept but
marked inert, so it no longer reads as protection.

**Plant gain, measured.** `heater_ctl.h` says the gain "cannot be characterised
anywhere but in a real pile", so the shipped kp/ki are placeholders. A fixed
4.00 % duty step gives a first bench number:

```
baseline 26.02 C -> settled 30.06 C over 300 s
=> 1.01 K per % duty, tau ~ 77 s (the cooling curve gives ~62 s)
```

+2 K therefore needs about **2.0 % duty (~21 mW)**. This is the whole-board
rise in still air with NO ring fitted, measured at the colocated sensor pair;
with a ring and with airflow in a pile the path is different and the gain will
be much lower. Treat it as the starting point for tuning, not as the plant.

**Side result: the ADS1119 stale-DRDY drain is verified on hardware.** An
aborted I2C transfer during this session stranded an unread conversion, and the
pre-`drain_stale_result()` firmware locked the oxygen channel out permanently
(`faults 0x8005`, VREF sentinel) exactly as that fix's comment predicts. With
the fix flashed, a deliberately stranded result - DRDY confirmed high, then a
START nobody reads, DRDY confirmed low - is recovered with no reset and no
fault. That is the known-bad calibration for that guard, on silicon.
- Measure temperatures immediately on both sides of the transverse barrier
  during at least three steady heater-power steps. Treat
  `delta_T / P_electrical` only as a lower bound; qualify the PCB value with
  body-side heat-flow measurement or a guarded loss correction as specified
  in `THERMAL-BARRIER.md`.
- Run controlled dew-point crossings and humid soak tests. Demonstrate that
  the membrane stays above local dew point without overheating or biasing the
  electrochemical cell; validate the condensation model against measurements.
- Complete TMP117 calibration, VREF/noise tests, N₂/air/30% O₂ calibration,
  and potting coupons.
- Clear `audit_bom.py` before ordering parts, not merely before production
  PCBA. This gate previously read "freeze exact procurement MPNs before
  production PCBA", which left hand-built first-article purchasing uncovered:
  L1, DZ1 and F1 were bought by nobody because nothing checked. The audit
  resolves every fitted component to an exact MPN or an explicitly declared
  class in `sourcing.json`, and diffs the result against
  `purchase-orders.json`.
